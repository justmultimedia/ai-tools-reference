Married couples need to be careful when making their will. Many married couples out there who have a will, which isn't many because only 27% of you have wills according to the Law Society, but those of you who do and those of you who are married may well have a mutual will. A mutual will is a joint will effectively. Let's say that will says I leave everything to you, you leave everything to me and equally to the kids if we're both dead. The place to be careful here is if one partner dies and leaves the other behind. Because the other behind has an implied agreement with the deceased. They're not able to change their will. So even though the will says, let's say, I leave everything to my kids, and even if the kids have revolted against the surviving parent and they want to change that, it's not possible. So be careful. Even though mutual wills might be cheaper because you have one will instead of two, it may be more prudent for you to consider two wills. So that on the death of one partner, the remaining partner has all options open to them.
