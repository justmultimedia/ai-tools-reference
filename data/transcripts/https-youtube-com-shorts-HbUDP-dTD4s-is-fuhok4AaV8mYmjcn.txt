Title: The Story of how Fatboy Slim made Right Here, Right Now
Channel: El Train | Loop Kitchen
Transcript: published captions

There aren't many records as iconic as Fatboy Slim's right here, right now. &gt;&gt; [music] &gt;&gt; But did you know it might never have existed without Massive Attack and a '90s sci-fi movie? &gt;&gt; I was reading a DJ Magazine, the best dance singles of all time, and it was Unfinished Sympathy by Massive Attack. And I was thinking, what is it about that tune that makes it so good? I was thinking, it makes you emotional. It's those strings. And so, I thought I just want to make a string track. I just started going through my discs and it's like string loops. I think with the main two are off a record called The Ashes the Rain and I by the James Gang. I just took little loops of them and overlaid them so and they just had this kind of stirring emotional sound, which is what I wanted out of strings. And more importantly, I couldn't afford to hire an orchestra. &gt;&gt; And then there's that oh so familiar vocal refrain. That came from a new trick that Fatboy Slim had been using on the album. &gt;&gt; One thing is that I really enjoyed discovering on that second album is I worked out that you can make a rhythm out of something that someone said. If you hear them say it and you think, "Oh, there's a little bit of rhythm in that." You can chop it into little pieces and make a proper rhythm out of it. &gt;&gt; And that's exactly what he did for the vocal on Right Here, Right Now, taking a line of dialogue from the 1995 film Strange Days. &gt;&gt; THIS IS YOUR LIFE, RIGHT HERE, RIGHT NOW. IT'S REAL TIME. YOU HEAR ME? REAL. RIGHT NOW, RIGHT HERE, RIGHT NOW, RIGHT HERE. &gt;&gt; [music] &gt;&gt; And that's only scratching the surface of Norman Cook's story.
